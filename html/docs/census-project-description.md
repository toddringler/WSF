# A Census of NGO Actions to Protect Wild Steelhead

*Draft project description.*

## Purpose

Create a freely accessible, web-based census of actions by nongovernmental organizations intended to protect wild steelhead: a documented, range-wide answer to the question of what the NGO community is doing, where, and against which threats. The census will provide the baseline for examining how conservation actions align with the biological risks wild steelhead face, and a practical resource for finding relevant work and expertise.

## Motivation

A first step toward credibly identifying gaps between the biological risks facing wild steelhead and conservation actions is answering a basic question: what is the full scope of NGO work to protect wild steelhead? Without a reasonably complete answer, a claimed gap is only an impression. A thorough census also protects any one organization from being judged on an incomplete picture of the entire community's effort.

Answering that question is harder than it sounds. Wild steelhead conservation is carried out largely watershed by watershed, by many organizations working on different parts of the range and the life cycle. Each organization knows its own work well, but it can be difficult to see the whole: which threats are receiving attention, where, and from whom.

I am starting with nongovernmental organizations because they often connect scientific knowledge to conservation action and can respond quickly to emerging needs. Their work offers a useful window into how conservation priorities are being translated into action on the ground.

## Initial Scope

The census will take a range-wide perspective, beginning with a manageable set of organizations and actions. Its geographic coverage, sources, and limitations will be clearly stated.

Actions could include research, monitoring, habitat restoration, policy advocacy, litigation, and public engagement. The census records what organizations are doing with the intent of protecting wild steelhead. It does not presume that any particular action helps. Whether a given action benefits wild steelhead is a question the census can help others examine, not one it answers. Inclusion does not imply endorsement or demonstrated effectiveness.

The initial census will focus on actions currently underway. Each entry will record its status and when its sources were last reviewed. Completed actions may be included where they provide essential context, but will be clearly distinguished from ongoing work.

## Which Organizations Are Included

The census uses a simple test. An organization or activity is included if it is a U.S. 501(c)(3) nonprofit or a Canadian nonprofit organization, including registered charities; if it is made up of such organizations (such as a coalition); or if it operates under one (such as a fiscally sponsored project). Organizations that do not meet this test are outside the scope of this census.

Tribes and First Nations are sovereign governments. Their conservation actions, like those of federal, state, provincial, and local governments, fall outside this census and would be considered alongside other governments in any future work. Native-led nonprofits that meet the test above are included.

Organizations will be identified starting from known groups and expanding through the organizations they partner with, fund, or join in coalitions. Each decision to include or exclude an organization will be documented.

## What Counts as an Action

An action is a distinct project, program, or policy effort that an organization undertakes to protect wild steelhead. Each entry will describe what is being done, where, and which biological threat it addresses. An organization’s general mission or statement of concern will not, by itself, count as an action.

## Approach

The census will be built from publicly available documents, including organizational websites, project descriptions, reports, and other published materials. Each entry will cite its sources. Where those materials do not establish an action’s status or scope, the uncertainty will be made explicit.

- Examine existing directories, databases, and related work before defining what the census will add.
- Establish a consistent structure for recording organizations, actions, geography, life stages, and biological threats addressed.
- Document sources and the date information was reviewed; distinguish reported information from WSF interpretation.
- Populate an initial version. Invite every organization in the census to review its own entries, and invite all users to identify errors, omissions, and useful improvements. Corrections must point to publicly available documents, so that anyone can verify the census.

## Initial Outputs

A working version of the census as a searchable website, accompanied by a description of its methods, coverage, and limitations.

A companion summary will describe the initial coverage, patterns in documented conservation actions, and questions that warrant further examination. It will distinguish gaps in the census from potential gaps in conservation activity; an action missing from public documentation cannot be assumed not to exist.

Software will be released under an open-source license, and original documentation and database materials under appropriate Creative Commons licenses, respecting restrictions on third-party material.

## What I Will Test

Does bringing this information together help people find relevant expertise, understand the conservation landscape, or identify needs that merit closer examination? Feedback and examples of use will help determine whether the resource warrants further development.

## Next Steps

The next step is to develop a detailed project plan defining the initial coverage, methods, database structure, and software requirements. That plan will guide development of a first version for community use and feedback and will be made available here. I expect the first version to be available in spring 2027.
